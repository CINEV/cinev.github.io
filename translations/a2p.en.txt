01 / A structure for repeatable production
Re-create while preserving
the story and style.
We analyzed why storyboards compressed events and directing information, and outlined a structure that separates story planning from image and video rendering.
Intended production experience
We aimed to let users compare results from the same story plan, produce only the parts they need, and repeatedly apply their own styles.
Shared plans · Selective rendering
For Lazy Storyboard V2, we documented specifications and integration directions for sharing plans and separating rendering. Video generation itself was not first introduced at this stage.
Reusable custom styles
We outlined a structure for converting user descriptions into prompts for each generation stage, registering them, and reusing them through an assigned style ID.
Observed results
Two tests identified the fixed nine-shot limit and equal allocation across scenes as the main causes of compression. Even shot-heavy climaxes received the same shot budget, losing directing information.
Remaining limitations
This stage primarily consists of design, specification, and integration records. No measured improvements in cost, user satisfaction, or story retention were reported, so the plan-based structure cannot be said to have solved the compression problem.
Next direction
The work points toward comparing generated results from the same story plan and selectively revising and producing parts of it.

User testing indicated that visual composition editing was more intuitive than text instructions. This was qualitative feedback, not a measurement of satisfaction or usability improvement rates.
Occluded areas, complex interactions, and large viewpoint changes remained unstable. Reconstruction errors also affected generation results, so manual correction was necessary.
Rather than focusing solely on perfect reconstruction, we emphasized a workflow users could correct and iterate on.

04 / Connect to a storyboard
Enlarge storyboard using generated elements: The Shibuya Save

The goal was to carry the desired appearance and spatial composition across scenes and reuse prepared assets.
Elementalist · Reference composition
We tested character generation combining face, top, bottom, and hairstyle references; location-object combinations; text-based prop generation; and connections to the storyboard.
Improved scene references
We reflected character visibility in generation and fixed an issue where the default background was referenced instead of the generated one. We analyzed why A-poses persisted and improved the prompts.
We verified the feasibility of character and prop replacement and reuse of existing 3D backgrounds. In a validation shot, regenerating the same input resolved the A-pose while preserving composition.
Identifying three or more people, background detail consistency, complex interactions, and contact with floors and chairs remained unstable. Prompt improvements did not solve upstream motion-application issues.
Alongside better reference transmission, we explored editing tools that let users directly control the scene itself.
04 / Testing feasibility
How far could AI go
in video production?
We tested whether AI could create the characters, backgrounds, motion, and voices needed for video and connect them into a single production workflow.

The unsuccessful experiment recorded character mix-ups and unwanted people appearing. These images are a collection of shots from the experiment, not individually annotated error examples.
We aimed to reduce repetitive production tasks and reuse prepared characters and scenes in other content.
Character and scene generation
We experimented with background editing and sequential character replacement, validating a workflow from images to video.
Motion and audio generation
We experimented with dialogue-aligned motion and voice generation, as well as story-based background music.
Success rate for dummy character removal
Share of final clips with no defects
Results from the same 108 clips evaluated under two different success criteria.
In a test of 108 clips, success measured by dummy character removal was 87%, while the share of final videos with no defects was 56.5%. Being able to generate something and being able to use it directly in production were different standards.
Limitations remained in overlapping characters, body proportions, pose reproduction, and background consistency across camera angles. Unwanted people appearing in video could not be fully prevented.
We expanded the research beyond individual generation functions toward a production workflow that carries the same characters and spaces across multiple shots.
